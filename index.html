Season preview

Meet Coach Trenk: A Pennsylvanian, a Plan, and the Guys Up Front

By the Bayou Report staff

MONROE, La. — The new head coach of the ULM Warhawks arrived in the Louisiana heat wearing a Pitt windbreaker, carrying a three-ring binder, and asking where a man could find a decent cheesesteak. Somebody handed him a plate of boudin instead. He is still deciding how he feels about it.

Charles Trenk, Coach Trenk to every player, trainer, and equipment manager in the building, inherits a program coming off a 3-9 season, with a conference mark of 1-7. He does not pretend otherwise. "We're not hiding from last year," he told the room at his first team meeting. "We're building on top of it. Possibly underneath it. We'll see."

A players' coach with a type

Ask the players about Trenk and the answers come fast. He knows their names, their hometowns, and the names of their little brothers. He eats in the cafeteria. He is the kind of coach who will walk across a practice field to check on a freshman who looks lost.

Ask him about football, though, and you get a speech about the trenches, delivered by a man who played both lines in high school and never really came back from it. "Football is won by the people nobody takes pictures of," he says. The offensive line is the most important group on the field, in his opinion, and he'll say it again if you let him.

His plan is to rebuild from the lines out, which sounds poetic until you read the roster. Most of the starting line is seniors, including tackle A.J. Vinson, guard Kaliem Croswell, and center Sean Dugery, and the defensive front has its own senior core. "Those guys get my best year," Trenk says, "and I get theirs." Whether that's a promise or a threat, he won't say.

The quarterback question

Redshirt junior Aidan Armenta and sophomore Austin Carlisle spent camp trading reps and trading looks. The staff talks about "competition" and "we like both guys." The quarterback room has had the kind of tension that makes practices interesting and film sessions awkward. Whoever wins the job, the other is going to matter.

Playmakers

This is where the roster gets fun. Nic Trujillo and Parker Fulghum have been fighting over who's the top receiver since spring, and veteran Brandon Buckhaulter would like it noted that he's right here. The backfield is deep, with Derrick Jameson, Donald Chaney, and young Jaydon Wright. On defense, senior middle linebacker Noah Flemmings is the one every teammate points to, and kicker Max Larson is expected to get plenty of work.

The road ahead

The Warhawks open with back-to-back road games at Charlotte and UAB, then come home for an FCS opponent and Florida Atlantic before a trip to South Alabama. After a week off, the Sun Belt grind begins: Louisiana Tech at home, then Troy, Southern Miss, a trip to Arkansas State, and the Ragin' Cajuns at Malone Stadium. App State on the road and Marshall at home close the season. That's three rivalry games in five weeks, which is either a gift or a trap.

Most preseason picks have ULM near the bottom of the conference. Trenk says he hasn't read them, then quotes one.

Player to watch

A.J. Vinson, RT. Ask Coach Trenk who his favorite player is and he'll say the whole roster. Ask who's second and watch where he looks.

Quote of the week

"I love every one of these guys. Some of them just don't get their names in the paper, so I say them extra." — Coach Trenk

Laissez les bons temps rouler, cher. Trenk would like them to roll through the A-gap.

Season preview

New Era in Tampa: A Retired Army Recruiter, a Roster Full of Seniors, and a Week 2 Trip to West Point

By the Bulls Report staff

TAMPA, Fla. — It's 90 degrees at kickoff, the humidity is doing what it does, and the USF Bulls are among the favorites to win the American Athletic Conference.

Leading them is a man who knows something about discomfort. Chris Truffa, a Vandergrift, Pennsylvania, native, retired from the Army this year after a career that ended with him in charge of recruiting. Weeks later he was running a Division I football program coming off a 9-4 season and a fifth-place finish in the American.

Players describe him as a leader of men who runs a disciplined operation: early meetings, tight schedules, and a chain of command he expects everyone to respect. "He doesn't raise his voice," one player said. "He doesn't have to."

He also spent years convincing young people to sign up for something hard. Recruiting in Florida, he says, feels like a day off. "Selling a kid on Tampa and the beach is easier than the job I did before," Truffa said.

His newest signee has been tougher on the schedule. Truffa's infant son, Cooper, has the coach running on very little sleep. "Best signing I've made," he said. "Worst hours."

What he inherits

It's a veteran roster. Right guard Cook and left guard Shrader anchor a seasoned offensive line, and the receiving room is five deep: Odom, Rodgers, Reuben, Seldon, and Branch. Junior quarterback Van Buren Jr. is the starter, with Kromenhoek pushing from behind.

The running back spot has drawn the most attention in practice. The depth chart lists sophomore Isaac ahead of senior Crowther, and Truffa's explanation was short. "Everybody earns it, every week."

On defense, Hicks is the pass-rushing end, with Simmons Jr., Redwood, and Dillard inside. Bryan and Fortenberry anchor the corners, and Howard calls the defense from middle linebacker.

Week 2 is personal

The first big test is a conference trip to Michie Stadium at West Point to face Army. Truffa spent his career in that uniform. Now he'll be on the opposite sideline.

"I owe everything to that uniform," he said. "And I'm still going to try to win."

Circle Week 7

Kent State comes to Raymond James Stadium in Week 7, and the visiting sideline will have a familiar face on it: head coach Rosco P-Coletrain, Truffa's best friend since college.

Coletrain has a doctorate in psychology, has never played a down of football, and runs one of the most unconventional offenses in the Mid-American Conference. The two have been trading texts since the schedule came out.

"He's my best friend," Truffa said. "For three hours on a Saturday, he's the enemy. Then we eat."

Coletrain was less restrained. "I've spent years studying how people behave under pressure," he said. "Chris is about to be a very interesting case study."

The stretch run is tough: Memphis at home, a trip to Florida Atlantic, and Tulane at home to close the regular season, all with the American title in play.

Player to watch

Hicks, defensive end. Quarterbacks have been looking over their shoulders in practice.

Quote of the week

"Discipline is doing it when nobody's watching. Football is the Army with better weather." — Coach Truffa

The Professor Comes to Kent: A PhD, a Gimmick Offense, and a Schedule That Doesn't Care

By the Dix Stadium Dispatch staff

KENT, Ohio — Let's get this out of the way. Kent State just hired a head coach with a doctorate in psychology who has never played a down of football and runs an offense that looks like a pop quiz. In the Mid-American Conference. The league of three yards and a cloud of dust, and Tuesday nights in November when your fingers go numb.

I was skeptical. So were the people in the press box at Dix Stadium.

His name is Rosco P-Coletrain. He's from West Newton, Pennsylvania, he went to Middle Tennessee State, and the people who know offense say he's one of the sharpest young minds in the game. The people who know the MAC say they say that about everybody.

Then you sit through a practice.

The offense

It is, to be fair, creative. Gimmicky is the word you'll hear from the traditionalists. One package he calls "Cognitive Dissonance" is a run that looks like a pass and a pass that looks like a run. The quarterbacks say it's the best thing they do. The linemen say it's the hardest thing to explain.

Coletrain is full of passion, and a little cocky, and he says so himself. He needed a program that would let him install all of it. Kent State needed a coach. Both sides are calling it a fit.

His wife, Julia, is a regular at Dix Stadium. So is Wallace, the family dog, who has been spotted at practice, mostly supervising.

What he inherits

A team that went 5-7 last year and finished seventh in the MAC. Junior quarterback DeShields leads a room with two younger quarterbacks behind him, Ruffin and Thanthanavong, which means options, and options are the entire point.

Sophomore receiver Harris is the headliner, with senior Clark, Banks, and Etheridge for company. The backfield is crowded: King, Caffey, Delaney Jr., and another Harris. At tight end, Mulder leads the way.

Up front, the line is young but not green: senior Morell at left guard, senior Adewole at right tackle, sophomore Meadows at left tackle, and sophomore Dunn at right guard. On defense, Aden anchors the tackles, Nicholl runs the middle, and Muhammad is the veteran in the secondary. Both the kicker, Leonard, and the punter, Stoeckel, are freshmen.

The road

The schedule doesn't care about his PhD. The Flashes open at South Carolina. Week 3 is a trip to Columbus to face No. 1 Ohio State. Then the MAC grind: Ball State and Ohio at home, at Western Michigan, Akron at Dix in a rivalry game, a trip to Sacramento State, at Bowling Green, Miami (Ohio) at home, and a closer at Eastern Michigan. Two open weeks. The conference title game, if they get there, is at Ford Field.

Week 7

Circle it. Kent State goes to Tampa to face USF, and the other sideline belongs to Chris Truffa, Coletrain's best friend since college.

"I've spent years studying how people behave under pressure," Coletrain said. "Chris is about to be a very interesting case study."

I'm still skeptical. But I've covered this league long enough to know the weirdest thing you can do in the MAC is win with something nobody's seen.

Player to watch

Harris, WR. The headliner of the experiment.

Quote of the week

"I never played the game. Maybe that's why nobody told me what's not allowed." — Coach Coletrain

Three Friends, Three Programs: The Upstate's Week 0 Roundup

By the Upstate Wire staff

GREENVILLE, S.C. — If you've lived in the Upstate long enough, you know how this goes. Somebody you grew up with moves away, takes a coaching job, and suddenly you're checking box scores for a team in a state you couldn't find on a map. Three of those coaches start their seasons this month. Here is where each one stands.

UL Monroe: a rebuild from the trenches

Coach Charles Trenk opens at Charlotte, a couple of hours up I-85 from here, with a quarterback job he hasn't settled and a plan to rebuild the Warhawks from the lines out. He loves his linemen and says so often. Three rivalry games in five weeks are waiting behind the opener.

Next up at Charlotte, Week 1.

USF: from the Army to the sideline

Coach Chris Truffa retired from the Army, where he ran recruiting, and now has the Bulls among the favorites in the American. He hosts FIU in Week 1, then travels to West Point in Week 2 to face his old branch. A newborn at home is keeping his sleep schedule short.

Next up vs. FIU, Week 1.

Kent State: the one headed to our side of the map

This is the one with a Palmetto State angle. Coach Rosco P-Coletrain, a psychology PhD who never played football, opens at South Carolina in Columbia, a little under two hours down the road, then goes to No. 1 Ohio State in Week 3. His offense is creative. The traditionalists call it gimmicky.

Next up at South Carolina, Week 1.

The Friends Game

In Week 7, Kent State visits USF in Tampa. The two head coaches have been best friends since college, and neither is pretending to go easy. Pencil it in, Taylor.

Game to watch

Kent State at USF, Week 7.

  • UAB and Florida Atlantic play both UL Monroe and USF. Bowling Green plays both USF and Kent State.
  • Coaches Trenk and Coletrain grew up together in West Newton, Pennsylvania. Coach Truffa is from Vandergrift.
