Week 11 recap

Three Seconds Left, One Pick-Six, and a Rivalry Settled Early: ULM Buries Louisiana 48-17

By the Bayou Report staff

MONROE, La. — There were three seconds left in the first quarter when Louisiana's quarterback threw the ball toward the wrong jersey. Jabari Tiller, ULM's strong safety, grabbed it, ran 38 yards the other way, and put the Warhawks up 17-10 on a Thursday night at Malone Stadium. By the time Louisiana scored again, in the fourth quarter, ULM led 48-10, and the evening had long since been decided. ULM won 48-17.

It was one of two interceptions for Tiller, and the Sun Belt noticed: the league named him its Defensive Player of the Week. Jayden Lewis picked off two more, and Louisiana, which came in 3-6, threw five in all. The rivalry did not look like much of a rivalry once the football started changing hands.

Austin Carlisle was back in the huddle after the shoulder injury that sent him to the sideline at Arkansas State, and the offense looked like it remembered him. He ran for two touchdowns and threw for two more, including a 32-yard strike to Parker Fulghum in the fourth quarter. He also threw two interceptions, which Coach Charles Trenk would prefer he forget by Monday. The line allowed three sacks, and Carlisle stood up after every one.

Trenk did not want to talk about the rivalry, which tells you what the rivalry meant to him. "It's just another game," he said, to a room that did not believe him. "Were we nervous? No. We were angry. There's a difference."

Odell Fontenot sold cracklins to fans of both schools at the Malone Stadium gate, which he regards as diplomacy. "Mais, I don't pick sides before kickoff," Fontenot said. "After the third quarter, I pick the winner."

The Warhawks are 8-2 and travel to Appalachian State next. The Mountaineers are 10-0 and ranked 21st, and they beat Marshall 21-14 on Saturday. Trenk was asked whether his team was ready for a ranked opponent. "Ten and oh is a number," he said. "It isn't a team. We'll go find out which one we're playing." Allons.

Week 10 recap

Protect the Kid: ULM Answers a Quarterback Injury and a Bad Week With a 33-13 Win at Arkansas State

By the Bayou Report staff

JONESBORO, Ark. — Austin Carlisle was still on the ground, holding his shoulder, when Coach Charles Trenk walked past the trainers and found his starting offensive line. Carlisle had been hurt on a scramble in the second quarter, and Trenk gathered the five linemen on the sideline before anyone had finished helping him up. "I told them, that's your quarterback now," Trenk said. "He's young. He's learning. Go protect him."

Aidan Armenta, the young quarterback who took the snaps from there, held up his end. He threw a 20-yard touchdown to Brandon Buckhaulter and strung together enough good throws to make the afternoon feel like a promotion, with two mistakes in the mix. "He played well," Trenk said. "Two mistakes, a lot of good throws. That's a kid who can play."

The defense made sure he never had to play from behind. ULM led 23-3 at the half, and the play that tilted the day came in the second quarter, when safety Chris Payne stepped in front of a pass and returned it 32 yards for a touchdown. Max Larson kicked four field goals, the longest from 49 yards. Arkansas State finished with 211 yards and converted two of 13 third downs, and Larell Howling made 11 tackles, four of them for loss.

It had been a quiet week in Monroe. Trenk promised his players a different kind of Monday after the 52-38 loss to Southern Miss, and it turned out to be a silent one: no chatter, no jokes, perfection demanded on every rep, and players who knew better than to test him. His headset, thrown a week earlier, stayed on his head all afternoon.

The line allowed four sacks, but Trenk was careful about the accounting. Three, he said, were the young quarterback holding the ball, and the fourth was the scramble that cost Carlisle his day. "Three of them were on the kid for holding it," Trenk said. "I'll tell him that. He'll hear it again."

Carlisle was undergoing tests on the shoulder at the time of this writing to determine how serious the injury is, though the early word was encouraging.

Lucille Hebert has sold programs outside the Malone Stadium gate since Reagan was in office, and she watched the huddle on the sideline from a television in the back of her stand. "I've seen five coaches. Never saw one grab his big men like that," Hebert said. "When a coach does that, the quarterback knows somebody has his back."

The Warhawks are 7-2 and host Louisiana next, and the Bayou Report will not pretend this is an ordinary week. The Ragin' Cajuns are 3-5 overall and 2-2 in the conference through Week 9, which in this rivalry means nothing at all. Trenk was asked whether Carlisle would be back. "We're waiting on the tests," he said. "Whoever's in the huddle is going to be protected." Allons.

Week 9 recap

Six Touchdown Passes, Eight Sacks, and One Airborne Headset: Southern Miss Beats ULM 52-38

By the Bayou Report staff

MONROE, La. — The lead lasted 24 seconds. Max Larson's 37-yard field goal put ULM up 30-28 early in the third quarter, and Malone Stadium had barely finished believing in itself when Southern Miss answered with a 63-yard touchdown pass. The Warhawks never saw the front again. A Southern Miss team that arrived at 2-5 left with a 52-38 win, and Coach Charles Trenk finished the afternoon without a headset, having thrown his after yet another breakdown in coverage.

The defensive line never touched Southern Miss quarterback Ethan Hampton, and he used all that time to pick the secondary apart. He threw six touchdown passes, four of them covering more than 40 yards, and his receivers kept running free behind coverage that was rarely where it was supposed to be.

The offensive line was worse, if that is possible, and Trenk was in no mood to say otherwise. ULM allowed eight sacks. The five linemen whose names Trenk wrote in marker on a laminated certificate in the locker room have been asked to consider the certificate a loan. "I'm not going to dress it up," Trenk said. "I'm disgusted. I laminated those names. Now I want a heat gun and a razor blade."

The game turned in two quick strokes. With ULM down 38-30, Southern Miss blocked a field goal. ULM's defense forced a fumble, Korian Wilson knocking the ball loose and Larell Howling falling on it, which put the ball back in Austin Carlisle's hands. Carlisle threw an interception, and Laquan Robinson returned it 72 yards for a touchdown. A chance to cut the deficit to five became a deficit of 15.

Carlisle was under heat all night, and it showed at the worst moments. He threw three interceptions, that one the most expensive. But he never stopped trying to make something happen, and his 81-yard touchdown run in the second quarter was the reason ULM was within a point at the half. Parker Fulghum caught a dozen passes and made the night's most reliable play, and Chris Payne's interception was most of the rest of the good news.

Marcus Thibodeaux, a graduate assistant who has spent the season charting the secondary, was most worried about what comes next. "I've been on this staff for two months and I have never seen Coach go quiet," Thibodeaux said. "He told us Monday would be a different kind of Monday. I asked what that meant, and he told me to bring a lunch. I'm bringing two."

The Warhawks are 6-2 and travel to Arkansas State in Week 10. The Red Wolves lost 44-42 in overtime to Georgia Southern in Week 8. Trenk was asked what he planned to do about the week ahead. "Monday," he said, and nothing else.

Week 8 recap

Four Picks, One Award, and 216 Yards From Roland: ULM Routs Troy 52-31

By the Bayou Report staff

TROY, Ala. — ULM scored three touchdowns in the first quarter, which is the kind of start that makes a long bus ride feel short. Troy answered with 17 straight points in the second quarter and trailed only 21-17 at the half, which is when the Warhawks remembered they owned a running back named Bryson Roland.

Roland carried 27 times for 216 yards and a touchdown, and ULM outscored Troy 31-14 after the break to win 52-31. The Warhawks gained 590 yards, 381 of them on the ground, and collected 39 first downs. Derrick Jameson scored twice and Jaydon Wright once on the ground. Brandon Buckhaulter caught six passes for 68 yards and two touchdowns, Nic Trujillo caught six for 90, and Parker Fulghum caught a touchdown too. Austin Carlisle added 85 rushing yards on 13 carries.

The defense took the ball away four times. Korian Wilson intercepted two passes, Jayden Lewis took one back 24 yards, and Carl Fauntroy picked off another. Wilson was named the Sun Belt's Defensive Player of the Week, the second Warhawk to win it this season. Terrelle Alexander had two sacks, Jaeden Calender had four tackles for loss, and ULM sacked Troy five times. Troy hit an 81-yard touchdown pass late, which made the final look closer than the game was.

There was one blemish. The offensive line, whose names sit in marker on a laminated certificate in the locker room, allowed four sacks, ending its sack-free streak at two games. "Four sacks," Coach Charles Trenk said. "I'd erase four names, but I laminated the thing. That was a mistake."

Odell Fontenot closed the cracklins stand at Malone Stadium and opened a second location in a parking lot in Troy, Alabama. "Mais, they had never had a cracklin," Fontenot said. "Imagine. A whole town, no cracklins. They have it now."

The Warhawks are 6-1 and return to Malone Stadium next week to host Southern Miss, which beat Louisiana 21-14 on Saturday. Trenk was asked how he would handle the line. "Linemen first," he said. "Lunch is on me again. The certificate stays laminated." Allons.

Week 7 recap

Five Extra Names on the Certificate: ULM Beats Louisiana Tech 34-20

By the Bayou Report staff

MONROE, La. — Austin Carlisle was named the Sun Belt's Offensive Player of the Week after the South Alabama game, and a certificate arrived to prove it. Coach Charles Trenk hung it in the locker room, took a marker, and added the names of the five starting offensive linemen, on the theory that a certificate is a team sport. On Saturday at Malone Stadium, the five names went to work. ULM beat Louisiana Tech 34-20.

The five men now immortalized in felt-tip did not allow a sack for the second time this season. ULM gained 552 yards, 295 of them on the ground, and led 19-6 at the half. Bryson Roland carried 11 times for 93 yards and two touchdowns, including a 73-yard run, the longest play of the Warhawks' day. Austin Carlisle completed 24 of 37 passes for 257 yards, threw a touchdown to Parker Fulghum, and added 72 yards on nine carries. Brandon Buckhaulter caught six passes for 65 yards, and Max Larson kicked a 47-yard field goal.

"Zero sacks again," Trenk said. "I'm having it laminated. Those names are not coming off."

The defense had Lazavier Amos. The middle linebacker made 10 tackles, four of them for loss, sacked the quarterback three times, and intercepted a pass for 27 yards. Larell Howling, the strong safety, forced a fumble and fell on it. ULM sacked Louisiana Tech five times, converted 12 of 20 third downs, and was flagged three times for 20 yards, which for this team continues to count as a clean sheet.

Odell Fontenot sold out of cracklins at the Malone Stadium gate before the second quarter. "Mais, I gave the cinder-block wall the day off," Fontenot said. "The line is on the clock now."

The Warhawks are 5-1 and head to Troy next. Troy has lost two straight, 45-23 to Southern Miss and 41-6 to Louisiana, by a combined 57 points. Trenk was asked whether that made for an easy week. "A team that has lost two in a row has spent all week thinking about it," he said. "That's the most dangerous kind." He declined to say what he was serving the linemen for lunch. Allons.

Lagniappe Week: ULM Takes a Bye, Buys Lunch, and Keeps Practicing

By the Bayou Report staff

MONROE, La. — A bye week is lagniappe, the little something extra the bayou hands you for free, and Coach Charles Trenk spent his the way he spends everything. He fed the offensive line.

The lunch was on him, as promised. Trenk bought the big men a spread of boudin and red beans and, as a nod to his Pennsylvania roots, a Pittsburgh-style sandwich with the french fries stacked inside. "Last week I told them to pack a lunch," Trenk said. "I owed them one, and I always pay what I owe the big men." The linemen, who did not allow a sack at South Alabama, ate in near silence, which Trenk says is the highest compliment a lineman can pay a sandwich.

Practice itself was brisk and short on drama. The Warhawks, 4-1, spent Monday and Tuesday on film and fundamentals and Wednesday on the scout team, where fullback Trevor Renz, fresh off two touchdown catches, was asked to play the part of Louisiana Tech's quarterback. "Renz played him with a lot of confidence and no arm," Trenk said. "It was perfect."

The quarterback room got equal time. Austin Carlisle and Aidan Armenta threw side by side all week, and Trenk declined to name a starter for Louisiana Tech. "The quarterback room is a room, not a ladder," he said, and then changed the subject to blocking.

In the locker room, the Sun Belt's weekly award certificates for Carlisle on offense and Carl Fauntroy on defense went up on the wall. Trenk then went down the line with a marker and added the linemen's names to Carlisle's. "A certificate is a team sport," he said.

Trenk also did his homework on the schedule. He watched Louisiana Tech lose 28-20 to Louisiana on Saturday, and he watched Troy, the Warhawks' opponent the week after, lose 45-23 to Southern Miss on Tuesday. "I watched both with the sound turned off," he said. "A game tells you more when nobody is talking over it."

With no game to sell at, Odell Fontenot took his cracklins to practice instead. "Mais, no game, no crowd," Fontenot said. "So I sold to the linemen. They called it research."

Louisiana Tech comes to Malone Stadium next. Trenk was asked how his team would arrive. "Rested, fed, and in possession of a certificate," he said. Allons.

Week 5 recap

Zero Sacks, 376 Rushing Yards, and a Very Hoarse Coach: ULM Rolls South Alabama 58-17

By the Bayou Report staff

MOBILE, Ala. — Coach Trenk said to ask him Monday, and Monday came with film attached. He watched it, and then he did what half of Ouachita Parish had been waiting to hear about. He stuck with Austin Carlisle.

Carlisle paid it back with interest. He went 25 of 34 for 192 yards and three touchdowns, threw one interception, and carried five times for 102 yards and two more scores, one of them a 40-yard run in the third quarter that made it 30-7. The Sun Belt named him its Offensive Player of the Week, a sentence nobody was writing seven days earlier. ULM beat South Alabama 58-17 on Saturday, and after a 7-7 first quarter the Warhawks outscored the Jaguars 51-10. Aidan Armenta, who played the fourth quarter last week, did not throw a pass.

"A man throws five interceptions on Saturday and goes 25 of 34 with three touchdowns the next Saturday," Trenk said. "That's a man who read his mail."

The mail was delivered by the men up front. The offensive line did not allow a sack, and ULM ran 54 times for 376 yards and finished with 36 first downs. Bryson Roland carried 21 times for 133 yards, Donald Chaney added 75, and fullback Trevor Renz caught two touchdown passes, which Trenk files under "linemen made that possible." Max Larson made all three of his field goals.

"Last week I said I'd love those guys loudly," Trenk said. "I'm hoarse. That's how you know it took."

The defense had its own good day. Carl Fauntroy, the Sun Belt's Defensive Player of the Week, intercepted two passes, returned them 33 yards, and recovered a fumble. Noah Flemmings made three tackles for loss and a sack, and Matthew Nicholson and Jayden Lewis each forced a fumble. South Alabama turned it over three times. ULM's two penalties cost 25 yards, which for this team counts as a clean sheet. With two players taking the conference's weekly honors, Trenk was quick to say where the credit started. "The offensive player of the week had an offensive line," he said. "I'd like that in the record."

Odell Fontenot closed his cracklins stand at Malone Stadium and drove seven hours to Mobile with a cooler. He sold out in the parking lot before kickoff. "Mais, I owe a cinder-block wall an apology, cher," Fontenot said. "Last week I said it held better than that line. This week the line held, and the wall got its job back."

The Warhawks are 4-1 and have next week off. They return to Malone Stadium in Week 7 against Louisiana Tech, which lost 22-16 to Army on Saturday. Trenk was asked how he planned to spend the bye. "Linemen first, same as always," he said. "Last week I told them to pack a lunch. This week I'm buying it." Allons.

Week 4 recap

Five Picks, Four Sacks, and One Very Angry Coach: Florida Atlantic 57, ULM 31

By the Bayou Report staff

MONROE, La. — Coach Trenk did not wait for the question. He sat down in the postgame room at Malone Stadium and said he was disappointed. Then he spent the next several minutes explaining the word.

Florida Atlantic beat ULM 57-31 on Saturday, and the scoreboard was the kindest thing about it. The Warhawks outgained the Owls 473 yards to 460 and ran 21 more plays. They also threw five interceptions, and two of them went back for touchdowns. FAU's Wilky Denaud returned one 20 yards for a score as the second quarter ran out, a play Trenk called a spirit breaker. In the third quarter, Joseph Sipp Jr. added a 35-yarder of his own.

"I'm not going to dress it up," Trenk said. "I'm disappointed in my offensive line, and I'm disappointed in my quarterback. Both of those sentences are true at the same time."

The line gave up four sacks, and two of the five interceptions came on hits to Austin Carlisle as he threw. Carlisle spent the afternoon fighting for his life. Trenk, who calls the offensive line the most important position on the field and has a soft spot for the men who play it, had nothing soft to say. "They played like it was the least important," he said. "I love those guys. I'm going to love them loudly all week."

He saved some for Carlisle, too. Two interceptions came with pressure in his face. The other three, Trenk said, did not. "You have to be willing to throw it in the Ouachita River," he said. "Nobody gets fired for throwing it in the river. Five interceptions is not an accident. It's a decision."

Aidan Armenta played the fourth quarter and went eight of 12 for 65 yards, finishing with a 29-yard touchdown pass to Bryce Anderson on the final play. Trenk was not in a celebrating mood. Asked whether Armenta would start at South Alabama, he said, "Ask me Monday, after I watch the film."

There were pieces worth saving. Jabari Tiller returned a fumble 32 yards for a touchdown, Noah Flemmings forced a fumble, Korian Wilson intercepted a pass, and Lazavier Amos made eight tackles. The flags stayed in the pockets, too: two penalties for 15 yards, and none at all for FAU. "Great," Trenk said. "We were disciplined while we got beat."

Odell Fontenot sold out of cracklins before halftime again, then stood at his stand outside the stadium and watched. "Mais, I got a cinder-block wall behind my stand that holds better than that line, cher," he said, "and it isn't even getting paid."

The Warhawks are 3-1 and next travel to South Alabama in Mobile. The Jaguars beat Ohio 41-6 in Week 3 and lost 35-14 at Kentucky on Saturday. Trenk was asked what he plans to do about the line this week. "Linemen first, Monday," he said. "Nobody leaves until I'm happy. Pack a lunch." Allons.

Week 3 recap

The Defense Runs the Show at Malone: Four Forced Fumbles and a 63-3 Home Opener

By the Bayou Report staff

MONROE, La. — Coach Trenk wanted his first home game to feel like home cooking, and the Warhawk defense served it hot. ULM beat FCS Southeast 63-3 at Malone Stadium on Saturday. The offense scored nine touchdowns, but the story was the other side of the ball: four forced fumbles, one returned for a touchdown, and a visiting offense held to 174 yards.

Odell Fontenot moved his cracklin stand to the Malone parking lot for the occasion and sold out before halftime. "Then I stood at the gate for the rest of it, cher, because nobody was leaving and nobody was buying," Fontenot said. "They didn't want to miss a fumble."

Five seconds of game clock separated Jaydon Wright's one-yard touchdown run from cornerback Jay Harper's 12-yard fumble return for another, and by the time the crowd sat back down, ULM led 14-0. FCS Southeast's only points came on a 38-yard field goal in the second quarter, with the Warhawks already up 21-0. They went into the half leading 28-3 and never looked back.

It was a team effort, and Trenk refused to pick a favorite. Middle linebacker Lazavier Amos led everybody with 13 tackles. Free safety Carl Fauntroy made 10 of his own, three of them behind the line. Defensive tackle Levontae Jacobs got the day's only sack. The visitors ran 40 times for 87 yards, completed 12 of 13 passes for another 87, and converted three of 14 third downs. They lost three of the four fumbles. "I can't give you one name, because I'd forget three," Trenk said. "Amos was everywhere. Fauntroy was everywhere else."

The offense did not just stand and watch. Austin Carlisle threw for 368 yards and two touchdowns, ran for 91 more, and capped the day with a 37-yard touchdown run with three seconds left. Brandon Buckhaulter caught nine passes for 171 yards, and Parker Fulghum added a six-yard touchdown catch. The running back committee ate well. Wright scored three times, Bryson Roland scored on a run and on a catch, Donald Chaney added a touchdown of his own, and Julian Walker returned six punts for 95 yards. Max Larson, who used up his one allowed miss last week, hit all nine extra points.

The one blemish was up front. Carlisle was sacked four times, which is a hard number for a coach who calls the offensive line the most important position on the field. "Four sacks is four conversations I plan to have with the big fellas," Trenk said. "Friendly ones. Loud, but friendly." The flags, at least, behaved. ULM was penalized three times after nine last week. "The sled is working," Trenk said, "and so is the part where they keep their hands inside."

The Warhawks are 3-0, and the second half was lagniappe. Next up is Florida Atlantic, back at Malone Stadium. KENT, Ohio — Here's what a season opener looks like when you're Kent State. You get on a bus, you ride to an SEC stadium, and somebody tells you the crowd will be loud. The Golden Flashes open Saturday night at South Carolina, 7 p.m. Eastern, and Coach Coletrain makes his debut.

I said I was skeptical. I still am. But I sat through practice this week, and the offense does not look like anything I've seen in this league.

Over eggs at a diner on Main Street, retired shop teacher Doris Kovacs, who has held Dix Stadium tickets since the Reagan administration, wasn't buying it. "I taught shop for thirty years," Kovacs said. "If somebody tells me an offense is going to mess with the defense's heads, I'd like to see the wiring diagram."

What the Gamecocks will see

Coletrain brought the diagram, sort of. "Cognitive Dissonance" is the package everyone's been whispering about, and he says it works because the defense has to hold two beliefs at once, run and pass, and the discomfort resolves in Kent State's favor. I wrote that down. I still can't tell you what it means. It looked like a screen pass.

There's more. Pre-snap motion, he says, is "priming." Overloading a linebacker with reads is "cognitive load." A defense that sees what it expects to see is committing "confirmation bias," and Coletrain intends to exploit it. He told the quarterbacks their reps are a "variable-ratio reinforcement schedule," which, as best I can tell, means nobody knows who's getting the reps next.

Junior quarterback DeShields is expected to run the show, with Ruffin and Thanthanavong behind him. Sophomore receiver Harris is expected to be the headliner, with Clark and Banks beside him and Mulder at tight end. Coletrain wouldn't confirm a depth chart. "Read it however you like," he said. The line is expected to be young but not green: Morell, Adewole, Meadows, Dunn.

The rest of it

Aden and Nicholl are expected to carry the defense, and Muhammad is the veteran in the secondary. Both specialists, Leonard and Stoeckel, are freshmen kicking in front of tens of thousands of strangers. Week 2 is a home game against an FCS team at Dix, then No. 1 Ohio State. Nobody said this was easy.

Around the conference, Eastern Michigan opened with a 30-14 win over Sacramento State. Kent State plays both, Sacramento State in Week 9 and Eastern Michigan in Week 13. Ball State, the Week 4 opponent, goes to No. 1 Ohio State this Saturday at noon, two weeks before Kent State makes the same trip. Coletrain plans to watch that film as "a natural experiment." He also says he is trying not to fall for "anchoring," the habit of letting the first number you see decide what you think. "Two data points are not a trend," he said. I wrote that down too.

Player to watch

Harris, WR. If the experiment works, he's why.

Season preview

The Professor Comes to Kent: A PhD, a Gimmick Offense, and a Schedule That Doesn't Care

By the Dix Stadium Dispatch staff

KENT, Ohio — Let's get this out of the way. Kent State just hired a head coach with a doctorate in psychology who has never played a down of football and runs an offense that looks like a pop quiz. In the Mid-American Conference. The league of three yards and a cloud of dust, and Tuesday nights in November when your fingers go numb.

I was skeptical. So were the people in the press box at Dix Stadium.

His name is Rosco P-Coletrain. He's from West Newton, Pennsylvania, he went to Middle Tennessee State, and the people who know offense say he's one of the sharpest young minds in the game. The people who know the MAC say they say that about everybody.

Then you sit through a practice.

The offense

It is, to be fair, creative. Gimmicky is the word you'll hear from the traditionalists. One package he calls "Cognitive Dissonance" is a run that looks like a pass and a pass that looks like a run. The quarterbacks say it's the best thing they do. The linemen say it's the hardest thing to explain.

Coletrain is full of passion, and a little cocky, and he says so himself. He needed a program that would let him install all of it. Kent State needed a coach. Both sides are calling it a fit.

His wife, Julia, is a regular at Dix Stadium. So is Wallace, the family dog, who has been spotted at practice, mostly supervising.

What he inherits

A team that went 5-7 last year and finished seventh in the MAC. Junior quarterback DeShields leads a room with two younger quarterbacks behind him, Ruffin and Thanthanavong, which means options, and options are the entire point.

Sophomore receiver Harris is the headliner, with senior Clark, Banks, and Etheridge for company. The backfield is crowded: King, Caffey, Delaney Jr., and another Harris. At tight end, Mulder leads the way.

Up front, the line is young but not green: senior Morell at left guard, senior Adewole at right tackle, sophomore Meadows at left tackle, and sophomore Dunn at right guard. On defense, Aden anchors the tackles, Nicholl runs the middle, and Muhammad is the veteran in the secondary. Both the kicker, Leonard, and the punter, Stoeckel, are freshmen.

The road

The schedule doesn't care about his PhD. The Flashes open at South Carolina. Week 3 is a trip to Columbus to face No. 1 Ohio State. Then the MAC grind: Ball State and Ohio at home, at Western Michigan, Akron at Dix in a rivalry game, a trip to Sacramento State, at Bowling Green, Miami (Ohio) at home, and a closer at Eastern Michigan. Two open weeks. The conference title game, if they get there, is at Ford Field.

Week 7

Circle it. Kent State goes to Tampa to face USF, and the other sideline belongs to Chris Truffa, Coletrain's best friend since college.

"I've spent years studying how people behave under pressure," Coletrain said. "Chris is about to be a very interesting case study."

I'm still skeptical. But I've covered this league long enough to know the weirdest thing you can do in the MAC is win with something nobody's seen.

Player to watch

Harris, WR. The headliner of the experiment.

Quote of the week

"I never played the game. Maybe that's why nobody told me what's not allowed." — Coach Coletrain
